# Xianxia master rules

Load this reference for every create, derive, optimize, diagnose, variants, or direct-image task. These are the controlling world and design rules shared by `单体仙境`, `神域聚居地`, `华彩通透仙侠`, `东方苍穹巨构`, and `仙界大境原典`.

Route references may expand these rules through scene topology, density, composition, palette, lighting, or atmosphere. They must not weaken, replace, or contradict them. Only an explicit user instruction may override a named master rule; preserve every unaffected rule.

## Measurable divine scale

- Prove scale through visible human comparisons, not adjectives alone.
- Include at least four readable scale proofs in every complete prompt: an off-frame giant fragment; adult-to-column, gate, opening, screen, plinth, tree, cliff, or bridge comparison; repeated elements converging into depth; ten to thirty body lengths of open space around a figure; vertically separated structures; a cloud-sea crossing; near-and-far giant forms; or an extreme-distance realm.
- When principal columns exist, make their diameter roughly five to ten adult heights and complete height roughly forty to eighty adult heights.
- When a principal opening, gate, screen, portal, or aperture exists, make it roughly twenty to fifty adult heights.
- When a principal eave, bridge, gallery, stair, platform, tree crown, cliff face, or city axis carries the scale, let it span hundreds of body widths, cross cloud layers, or continue beyond the frame.
- For an inhabited realm, map the same scale grammar onto one ruling district, tiny inhabitants, subordinate districts, connective infrastructure, and an extreme-distance settlement or higher heaven. Dense quantity never replaces measurable comparison.

## Fixed five-layer space

Build all five layers in every supported ratio and every route:

1. A near-camera giant frame or threshold: column, eave, screen, pine, cliff, gate edge, bridge start, terrace edge, or equivalent.
2. A broad human-bearing surface or lived threshold: platform, stair, gallery, bridge, street, garden court, cloud harbor, mirror ground, or open edge.
3. One ruling subject: a single landmark, principal palace district, city crown, divine axis, or cropped megastructure event.
4. One or more lighter subordinate groups: remote palaces, secondary districts, bridges, platforms, architectural fragments, mountains, or floating islands organized beneath the ruling subject.
5. A terminal celestial distance: extreme-distance settlement, higher heaven, giant moon, low sky light, heavenly river, low cloud sea, or boundless clear sky.

- For `单体仙境`, keep layer 3 as one localized anchor and layer 4 restrained.
- For `神域聚居地`, let layer 4 contain connected subordinate districts without competing with layer 3.
- For `东方苍穹巨构`, layer 3 may be a cropped or occluded spatial event rather than one fully visible object.
- Never collapse the five layers into a flat foreground/background split, one object in empty cloud, or a roof carpet without vertical separation.

## Content-bearing breathing space

- Preserve roughly 40–60% content-bearing breathing space in every route. It may consist of layered sky, cloud gaps, water, open courts, portal air, space beneath eaves, void between floating districts, distant light, or transparent atmosphere.
- Treat breathing space as spatial information, not blank canvas. Give it value changes, silhouettes, cloud edges, bridge crossings, distant structures, or light transitions.
- A dense inhabited realm may distribute the 40–60% air between, below, behind, and through its districts instead of reserving one empty block. Density must come from overlap and continuity, not from eliminating air.
- Keep near forms heavier, sharper, darker, and more detailed. Make distance lighter, softer, lower-contrast, and more generalized without turning gray.
- When a main cloud sea is present, keep it below people and their bearing surface unless the user explicitly requests immersion in cloud. Use local thin mist to separate layers; never wrap the entire realm in a gray veil.

## Heavenly architecture language

- Use Chinese spatial order without reconstructing a specific dynasty. Recompose extended eaves, colossal column fields, large openings, giant screens, corridors, heavenly stairs, layered roofs, cloud platforms, suspended gates, gardens, and bridges into a unique celestial language.
- Make the largest structures immense, simple, quiet, and structurally legible: large uninterrupted material planes, few organizing lines, repeated components for distance, and broad calm surfaces for weight.
- Concentrate intricate decoration in one or two human-readable zones. Derive large shallow reliefs from cloud flow, water movement, star tracks, moon halos, wind marks, mountains, or celestial arcs. Do not spread equal filigree across every giant surface.
- Preserve credible load paths, beam-column relationships, platform thickness, bridge support, roof construction, material weight, and perspective even when the world floats or defies gravity.
- Favor heavy pale stone, pearl-gray stone, weathered jade, restrained vermilion or ochre timber, dark bronze, mineral blue-green, narrow pale-gold edges, real water, and volumetric cloud. A route may change the palette but not the structural calm or material credibility.

## Railing rule

- Use no railing, balustrade, fence, parapet, guardrail, or decorative edge barrier on ground, platforms, stairs, bridges, galleries, terraces, cloud harbors, or architecture by default.
- Keep edges open and monumental. Define circulation through paving, level changes, columns, light, water, planting, or structural rhythm instead of railings.
- Add a railing only when the user explicitly requests one. A route, example, historical reference, safety assumption, or familiar composition must never introduce it automatically.

## Immortals and costume

- When people are present, default to fictional adults seen from the full back, a 30–60 degree rear angle, a side-rear walking view, or an over-shoulder rear view. Do not turn scale markers into front-facing portraits.
- Keep environment-led figures around 1–4% of frame height. Use 5–8% when the full silhouette must read. Use 12–20% only when explicitly requested for an over-shoulder or costume-led frame, while preserving scale through off-frame architecture and a complete distant realm.
- Give figures calm autonomous actions: slow walking, distant watching, ascending stairs, entering a gallery, crossing a bridge, or standing at an open edge. Preserve ten to thirty body lengths of open space around a principal scale-marker figure when composition permits.
- Apply `衣有骨、纱有气、饰有止、色有主`: resilient wind-catching lengths extend from shoulder, arm, or waist; two to four unequal rear hems lag behind movement; a floor-length robe and one controlled trailing cape, veil, or outer layer may carry the accent color.
- State one wind direction. Keep body and hair stable while hair, sleeves, hems, cape, and veil respond coherently to that same direction. Treat the costume as material evidence, not decorative chaos.
