# Composition, color, and light library

Select only what supports the scene after applying [xianxia-master-rules.md](xianxia-master-rules.md). This library may vary geometry, camera, palette, and light but may not reduce the fixed five layers, 40–60% content-bearing air, measurable scale, railing-free architecture, or immortal-costume grammar.

## Spatial skeletons

- **Strict one-point perspective**: Put one off-center or centered vanishing point in a deliberate location. Make longitudinal edges converge there; keep verticals parallel; reduce repeated spacing and scale consistently.
- **Centered symmetry**: Use for gates, palaces, ritual spaces, mirror lakes, and solemn scenes. Add one small asymmetry such as figures, birds, drifting cloth, or foliage to keep it alive.
- **Diagonal depth**: Lead from a large foreground element toward a smaller midground anchor. Use bridges, stairs, rooflines, shorelines, or light paths.
- **S-curve flow**: Use winding paths, rivers, cloud belts, bridges, or processions to reveal space gradually.
- **Framed vista**: Use doors, columns, branches, eaves, cliffs, or fabric at the edge to reveal a luminous distance.
- **Vertical ascent**: Use for 4:5 and 9:16. Lead from a small lower subject toward a tall gate, tower, waterfall, tree, or light opening.

For `东方苍穹巨构`, select the dominant spatial event from its dedicated reference. Treat framing, occlusion, and an architectural mass together as one geometry rather than adding several competing skeletons.

## Lens and camera routes

- **Wide environmental reveal (20–28mm equivalent)**: Use a large near frame, deep leading line, and distant landmark to exaggerate separation. Keep verticals controlled and prohibit fisheye distortion.
- **Balanced cinematic still (35–50mm equivalent)**: Use for believable architecture, natural figure scale, and less stylized perspective. Strengthen grandeur through scale bands and off-frame continuation rather than lens distortion.
- **Telephoto scale compression (70–100mm equivalent)**: Use a distant viewpoint to make an immense palace, gate, mountain, or moon dominate the horizon behind a small foreground figure. Separate planes through atmosphere and value, not wide-angle convergence.
- **Low camera height**: Use for stairs, gates, columns, and towers when upward dominance matters.
- **Elevated reveal**: Use only when the user explicitly prioritizes bird's-eye geography, city planning, district distribution, or island relationships over embodied pressure. Do not infer it from a cloud sea or city alone.

Do not combine extreme wide-angle distortion with strong telephoto compression in one frame.

## Camera priority for prosperity and oppression

When the user wants both a thriving celestial capital and megastructure pressure, choose in this order unless explicit camera instructions override it:

1. **Distant low viewpoint + 70–100mm compression**: default for dense rooflines, layered districts, and a colossal palace or gate looming behind them. Keep the viewer at terrace, bridge, or human eye height.
2. **Distant low viewpoint + 24–35mm rectilinear wide angle**: use for approaches, bridges, stairs, gates, column fields, and undersides. Keep enough camera-to-subject distance to avoid meaningless foreground distortion.
3. **Human-height threshold + 35–50mm**: use for doorways, terraces, cliffs, eaves, interiors, and framed revelations.
4. **Elevated overview**: use only for an explicit `城市总览` intent.

Do not confuse camera distance with camera height. A far camera can stay below the principal roofline and look slightly upward.

For `繁华压迫`, keep the horizon near the lower third, adult figures around 1–3%, a human-bearing threshold in the lower frame, dense subordinate districts across the middle, and an off-frame ruling mass above or behind them. Preserve 40–60% content-bearing air by interleaving sky, portal openings, cloud gaps, void beneath platforms, streets, courts, and atmospheric separation through the city; never trade the master breathing space for a solid roof carpet.

## Scale construction

### 辽阔

- Use all five master layers, four measurable scale proofs, a readable path into distance, and generous content-bearing sky, water, cloud, valley, or courtyard space.
- Figures may be absent or remain secondary.

### 史诗级 — default

- Use all five master layers and strengthen discontinuity between the near giant frame, known-size adult or threshold, ruling subject, subordinate realm, and terminal distance.
- Include at least four visible proofs, including one direct comparison such as adult-versus-gate, bridge-versus-palace, tree-versus-city, or waterfall-versus-island.
- Keep environment-led scale markers around 1–4% of frame height; use 5–8% only when the full silhouette must read.

### 超宏大 — macro-scale gate

Require all of the following unless the user explicitly overrides one:

1. All five master layers: near giant frame, human-bearing surface, ruling giant subject, subordinate realm, and terminal celestial distance.
2. Adult scale markers around 1–4% of frame height unless the user explicitly requests stronger narrative presence.
3. One colossal structure that continues beyond at least one frame edge or is partially occluded by a nearer frame.
4. Roughly 40–60% content-bearing breathing air distributed through sky, cloud sea, void, water, open courts, portals, or distance.
5. A visible extreme-distance layer rather than empty fog.
6. Four or more measurable scale proofs and clear value, contrast, or chroma steps between planes; do not place all subjects on one apparent plane.

Treat these as visual evidence, not words to paste mechanically. If the composition cannot satisfy the gate, label it `史诗级` instead of claiming `超宏大`.

## Focal hierarchy

Use three levels:

1. Dominant: spatial carrier or landmark.
2. Supporting: figures, secondary architecture, foliage, waterfall, or reflected form.
3. Texture: carvings, clouds, birds, petals, small lights, or distant roofs.

Do not assign equal contrast, scale, and detail to all three.

For `神域聚居地`, treat the principal palace district or divine axis as the dominant level, supporting districts as the second level, and repeated roofs, bridges, stairs, courtyards, cloud traffic, and small wonders as texture. Preserve density while controlling competition.

## Palette recipes

- **Clear celestial**: azure + jade white + vermilion + warm gold + dark wood.
- **Rain-washed garden**: emerald + peacock blue + coral + ivory + wet charcoal.
- **Bright nocturne**: cobalt + violet + restrained magenta + amber gold + moon white; keep blacks open and colored.
- **Sunlit mountain**: turquoise + pine green + cinnabar + apricot sky + pale stone.
- **Sacred dawn**: pale cyan + cloud white + saffron gold + mineral red + cool blue-gray shadow.

Aim for two dominant families, one high-chroma accent, and one neutral or dark stabilizer. Avoid using every palette color at equal strength.

## Saturation strategies

Use these as starting area budgets, then adapt to the subject:

- **自然鲜明**: 70–80% low-to-medium chroma base, 15–25% moderate chroma, up to 5% high-chroma accents.
- **选择性高饱和 — default**: 65–75% low-to-medium chroma sky/cloud/jade/stone, 15–25% moderate chroma structure and foliage, 5–10% high-chroma vermilion, coral, garment, flower, or gilded edge accents.
- **华丽高饱和**: Allow 15–25% high-chroma areas, but limit equally intense hue families and retain at least one large neutral or dark stabilizer.

Preserve material color separation:

- Keep white jade, cloud highlights, pale stone, and moon white close to neutral.
- Let warm sunlight affect exposed edges and planes; do not wash all materials with the same orange or gold.
- Keep cool shadow colors distinct from warm highlights.
- Reduce distant chroma and contrast progressively without turning distance gray.
- Keep gilding narrow and bright rather than spreading gold across roofs, clouds, skin, and ground.

## Lighting recipes

- **Low-angle golden backlight**: Warm directional light, bright material edges, cool interior shadows, one restrained starburst.
- **Rain-cleared daylight**: Crisp skylight, wet reflections, saturated foliage, soft sun break, minimal haze.
- **Cloud-gap illumination**: One shaft highlights the anchor while surrounding space remains luminous, not black.
- **Moon and lantern split**: Cool blue-violet ambient light plus localized amber light; preserve open shadow detail.
- **High-altitude morning**: Clean blue skylight, warm grazing highlights, volumetric cloud edges, transparent air.

## Material cues

- Jade: subtle internal scattering, mineral veins, polished edges, not uniform plastic.
- Lacquer wood: deep colored body, controlled specular highlights, fine grain at worn edges.
- Gilding: thin accents and raised-edge catches, never coating every surface.
- Bronze: dark patina, warmer worn ridges, crisp relief shadow.
- Glass or glaze: colored transmission, sharp edge highlights, believable thickness.
- Wet stone: darkened base value, broken reflections, localized sheen.
- Silk: directional folds, wind response, soft highlight rolloff.

## Failure corrections

- Dull image: increase high-key exposure, color separation, colored shadow detail, and one clear accent; reduce gray mist.
- Cheap neon look: reduce equal-intensity accents, restore ivory/stone/dark stabilizers, and localize the brightest chroma.
- Over-saturated or over-warm image: reduce the area of high-chroma red/gold, restore near-neutral jade and clouds, remove the global orange LUT, and confine warm spill to motivated lit planes.
- Large objects but weak grandeur: add discontinuous scale bands, reduce scale-marker figures, introduce an extreme-distance layer, let one structure continue beyond the frame, and choose either a wide reveal or telephoto compression route.
- Weak perspective: simplify geometry, name one vanishing point, specify repeated scale reduction, and remove competing axes.
- Clutter in `单体仙境`: retain one anchor, reduce decorative object types, and separate dominant/supporting/texture levels.
- Clutter in `神域聚居地`: retain one ruling district and connected city topology; simplify repeated module types and contrast hierarchy without deleting subordinate districts or urban continuity.
- Plastic architecture: add surface variation, carving depth, edge wear, translucency, patina, grain, and nonuniform reflection.
- Flat distance: create contrast and value steps across foreground, midground, background, and extreme distance.
- Excessive flare: keep one optically motivated flare at a backlit edge and remove decorative flares elsewhere.
- Generic megastructure: crop or occlude the main mass, restore an Eastern human-scale threshold, add a discontinuous scale comparison, reduce equal ornament across giant surfaces, and replace uniform gray grading with high-key warm/cool separation.
- Aerial-map feeling: lower the camera to human or terrace height, restore visible vertical faces and undersides, compress dense palace layers with 70–100mm from a distant viewpoint, lower the horizon, enlarge and crop the principal mass, and keep only an explicit overview request in an elevated view.
